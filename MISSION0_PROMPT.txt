# MISSION 0 — SCIENTIFIC BASELINE, LITERATURE AUDIT & RESEARCH FOUNDATION

## PROJECT

Repository: `C:\projects\Vision-Based-3D-Reconstruction`

Research objective:

**Convert large geographic areas captured by UAV photographs or video into geometrically meaningful, visually recognizable, textured 3D assets.**

This is an undergraduate Computer Engineering graduation research project.

We are NOT attempting to reinvent Structure-from-Motion, Multi-View Stereo, dense fusion, mesh reconstruction, or photogrammetric texturing.

We are allowed and encouraged to use established scientific implementations, including COLMAP, OpenMVS, and other appropriate open-source tools, subject to their licenses.

Our responsibility is to identify a defensible research gap, propose a genuinely meaningful contribution, and evaluate it experimentally.

The existing custom reconstruction implementation is not sacred. Preserve it for comparison, but do not keep it as the production backend merely because substantial effort was previously invested.

## MISSION OBJECTIVES

1. Establish an independent, visually credible reconstruction baseline using established photogrammetry software.
2. Audit the existing project and determine which components should be preserved, replaced, or repurposed.
3. Conduct a structured literature and novelty assessment.
4. Evaluate the proposed quality-guided regional adaptive reconstruction research direction against previous work.
5. Produce a scientifically defensible project architecture and research plan.

**Do not begin implementing the novel research algorithm during this mission.**

First establish a successful baseline and a well-supported research hypothesis.

# PHASE 2 — INDEPENDENT COLMAP BASELINE

## CRITICAL REQUIREMENT

Create a genuinely independent COLMAP reconstruction.

Do not import our custom SfM camera poses and call the result an independent COLMAP baseline.

COLMAP must estimate its own cameras from the source images.

Existing experiments using our camera poses are valuable controlled ablations, but they do not satisfy this requirement.

## 2.1 Environment inspection

Determine whether COLMAP is installed and usable.

Check:

- Version.
- GPU acceleration availability.
- CUDA compatibility.
- Available RAM and VRAM.
- CPU resources.
- Relevant executable paths.
- Existing COLMAP workspaces.

Avoid unnecessary reinstalls.

Do not modify system-wide settings without justification.

Use reproducible executable commands and configuration files.

## 2.2 Initial image connectivity analysis

Before running expensive dense reconstruction:

- Inspect source image resolutions.
- Examine EXIF/camera metadata.
- Generate a camera/image contact sheet.
- Determine probable image overlap.
- Identify distant camera clusters.
- Identify image acquisition patterns.
- Determine whether the full scene forms one geometrically connected camera network.

Do not assume sequential filenames imply neighboring camera views.

If image positions are available, use them for diagnostic mapping and initialization where appropriate.

Clearly identify whether positioning metadata is used in estimation, evaluation, or both.

Avoid data leakage into independent accuracy evaluation.

## 2.3 Full-scene independent SfM

Attempt independent COLMAP SfM for:

- Rural complete scene.
- Urban complete scene.

Use appropriate camera calibration and feature matching strategies.

Prefer established, reproducible configurations over arbitrary custom heuristics.

Inspect:

- Registered image count.
- Separate connected models.
- Camera graph connectivity.
- Reprojection errors.
- Camera intrinsics.
- Track lengths.
- Triangulation angles.
- Sparse point-cloud coverage.
- Camera-position plausibility.

If full-scene reconstruction fails or is computationally impractical, diagnose the reason and use a clearly documented coherent subset.

Do not silently substitute a smaller subset while reporting full-scene success.

## 2.4 COLMAP dense reconstruction

For successful SfM models, run COLMAP's established dense reconstruction pipeline.

Include:

- Image undistortion.
- Dense stereo/depth estimation.
- Depth-map fusion.
- Fused dense point-cloud export.
- Supported surface reconstruction methods.

Use standard, well-documented methods first.

Do not tune dozens of parameters without a specific diagnostic hypothesis.

Record configuration, runtime, hardware use, and intermediate outputs.

## 2.5 Surface reconstruction

Evaluate suitable reconstruction methods such as:

- Poisson surface reconstruction.
- Delaunay/visibility-based surface reconstruction.
- Alternative supported methods where justified.

Select methods based on scene structure and available geometric evidence.

Do not fabricate terrain or aggressively fill unsupported regions.

## 2.6 Photographic texture and asset export

Use an established compatible texturing/export route, including OpenMVS or another justified tool where appropriate.

Produce:

- A real photographic textured mesh.
- GLB and/or OBJ.
- PLY point clouds.
- Reproducible texture assets.
- Independent Blender import validation.

Document the precise software responsible for each stage.

Do not claim that COLMAP alone produced texture outputs if a separate package generated them.

# PHASE 5 — QUANTITATIVE BASELINE EVALUATION

Evaluate:

## SfM

- Registered camera ratio.
- Camera network connectivity.
- Reprojection error.
- Track statistics.
- Geometric plausibility.

## MVS

- Dense point count.
- Valid depth coverage.
- Spatial point density.
- View support.
- Reconstructed footprint.

## Mesh

- Triangle and vertex counts.
- Connected components.
- Largest component share.
- Spatial holes.
- Boundary edges.
- Non-manifold geometry.
- Sampled self-intersections.
- Supported reconstructed surface area.

## Geometry accuracy

Where reference data permits:

- Geometric precision.
- Geometric completeness.
- F-score at clearly defined metric thresholds.
- Common-footprint errors.
- DSM error statistics.
- Spatial coverage.

Distinguish full 3D accuracy from height-map-only accuracy.

Verify coordinate alignment, units, EPSG definitions, metric scale, and vertical datum.

Do not present offset-aligned DSM values as absolute geospatial accuracy.

## Runtime and resources

Record:

- SfM runtime.
- Dense reconstruction runtime.
- Meshing runtime.
- Texturing runtime.
- Export runtime.
- Peak RAM/VRAM where measurable.

Record hardware and configuration to make performance comparisons meaningful.

# PHASE 8 — RESEARCH NOVELTY AUDIT

Evaluate this candidate research direction:

**Quality-Guided Regional Adaptive Reconstruction of Large UAV Scenes under Computational Constraints**

Proposed research question:

Can an existing, partially successful textured 3D reconstruction guide the selection of additional already-captured images, local depth-processing strategies, and selective reconstruction effort to improve valid geometric completeness under a fixed computation budget?

Evaluate whether this is genuinely distinct from:

- Prioritized MVS depth generation.
- Adaptive image selection.
- Next-best-view selection.
- Quality-guided UAV acquisition.
- Adaptive meshing.
- Reconstruction uncertainty estimation.
- Incremental SfM/MVS.
- Regional refinement.
- Existing multi-resolution reconstruction methods.

Do not call the research idea novel merely because it combines existing algorithms.

Identify the exact technical contribution needed for defensible originality.

For example, a regional decision algorithm may jointly consider:

- Existing mesh coverage.
- Independent camera support.
- Expected improvement.
- Geometric uncertainty.
- Photographic texture support.
- Computational cost.
- Geometric consistency.

However, this is only a hypothesis until the literature and experiments support its originality and value.

Classify the candidate as:

- Clearly differentiated.
- Potentially differentiated but overlapping.
- Mostly established.
- Not yet assessable.

Provide evidence and explain the classification.

If the idea substantially duplicates previous research, propose up to three better-defined alternatives that retain the same core project objective.

Do not change the project goal.
